Why local matters

Margate hedges aren't inland hedges.

Thanet sits on Upper Chalk bedrock with a Thanet-Formation sand cap. Topsoil is thin (often under 15cm), alkaline (pH 7.5–8.5), and drought-prone, Margate posts around 1,577 sunshine hours a year, one of the sunniest and driest corners of the UK. Add a north-and-east-facing coast exposed to salt-laden onshore blasts and you have a growing environment that eats generic-nursery species alive.

I work to that reality, not against it. Griselinia and Escallonia on front-line seafront boundaries. Pittosporum and Euonymus in the shelter behind. Hornbeam instead of beech where beech would scorch. And a tape measure on any stem near the 75mm CA threshold before I touch it.

In a Cliftonville CA?

I handle the paperwork.

Almost all of Cliftonville sits inside one of Thanet DC's conservation areas, Dalby Square, Clifton Place & Grotto Gardens, Ethelbert Road & Athelstan Road, Edgar Road & Sweyn Road, Norfolk/Warwick/Surrey Road, Northdown Road, plus the Margate (1978, extended 1994), Margate Seafront (1997) and Northdown (1985) designations. Any tree with a stem over 75mm at chest height needs six weeks' written notice before pruning. I file the section-211 notice on your behalf, included in every quote.
