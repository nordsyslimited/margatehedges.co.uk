The changeover-window model for Margate holiday-let owners.

Since Turner Contemporary opened in 2011 and Dreamland reopened in 2015, Margate's short-term let stock has expanded to somewhere between 300 and 443 active listings depending on which platform snapshot you take. A lot of that stock is absentee-owned — London or Kent-inland owners who visit every few months. The garden is the least-managed part of the property. Here's how I actually work with the STR side of the fleet, so hedges get cut in the gap between bookings, not during them, and the owner never has to think about it.

The problem the changeover-window model solves

An owner-occupied hedge gets cut when the owner is home to open the gate and take the invoice. A short-term let hedge doesn't have that person around. Which means the standard hedge job either happens on a booked day (bad — guests hear a petrol trimmer at 10am, complain, one-star review), doesn't happen at all (worse — the front boundary grows out over the pavement, the seaward face goes ragged, the property presentation drops), or gets crammed into whatever window the cleaner has between checkout and check-in (worst — 40 minutes on a Saturday afternoon isn't a proper cut).

The working answer is to tie the trim schedule to the property's booking calendar. Not to my calendar. When there's a natural gap between bookings — even 24 hours between a Sunday checkout and a Tuesday check-in — that's when the hedge gets cut. Property empty, no guests to disturb, cleaner already coming through, easy access, no drama.

How it works in practice

  • Owner sends me the calendar. Airbnb, Booking.com, direct — whatever platform. I look for the natural gaps that fit within the two seasonal cut windows (late May/June and late August/September for formal hedges).
  • Key-safe or lockbox access. Owner leaves a key-safe or the standard Airbnb lockbox code with me. I don't need the owner to be present. This is the whole point.
  • Fixed slot in the gap. I book the cut for a specific date within the gap and confirm to the owner. If a last-minute booking closes the gap, I move the slot — but I always find another before the season closes.
  • Photo receipt. Job done, I send a before/after photo to the owner's phone same day. Cleaner arrives for the next changeover, hedge is done, no confusion about scope.
  • Recurring invoice. Standing arrangement, invoiced quarterly or per-visit depending on the owner's accounting preference. Bank transfer, no chasing.

The Margate booking-gap patterns I've learnt to look for

Early to mid-April: pre-season tidy before the Easter Dreamland reopening surge. Late May and June: the natural mid-week gaps between weekend-heavy bookings. Late August into September: post-peak, before the winter storm season begins. November to February: any gap. Winter is the honest maintenance window on the fleet; if a hedge needs a proper reduction, that's when it happens.

Neighbourhood-by-neighbourhood notes

Old Town lets

The stock around King Street, Market Place and the harbour is mostly listed or in the Margate CA. Hedge work is often small in scope — a low front hedge, a courtyard shrub line — but LBC and CA rules bite quickly. I file the s.211 notice for you where it applies. Parking is a nightmare; I schedule for early-morning arrivals to get a bay outside the property before the day-visitors do.

Cliftonville lets

The dense terrace stock along Dalby Square, Ethelbert Road, Athelstan Road, the Norfolk-Warwick-Surrey block. Most sit inside CAs. Front boundaries are small formal hedges, typically griselinia or escallonia, needing two cuts a year to hold line. Turnover is high, especially April to September. Gaps are usually mid-week.

Westbrook and seafront lets

Bigger gardens, more exposed to the salt wind. Hedges tend to be larger — front-line griselinia or escallonia catching the direct spray. Cuts take longer; I'll usually schedule a half-day and combine with any other Westbrook stock the same owner or manager has that week.

Palm Bay and eastern coastal estates

Post-war semi stock, garden hedges often leylandii inherited from the 1970s. Turnover slower than Cliftonville. If the leylandii needs to come down, we plan a proper reduction or removal in the November-to-February gap when the calendar clears.

Waste disposal, the boring bit that matters

Every STR job generates trimmings the guests should never see. I don't leave brash on-site to be dealt with by the cleaner. All arisings leave with me the same day. TDC's garden-waste subscription (£73 a year plus a one-off £44 bin hire) is fine for owner-occupied properties but useless for STR turnover — you can't wait a fortnight for the next collection when a booking arrives on Friday. My waste-carrier licence covers me to take everything to Manston Road HWRC directly. The bin at the property stays empty for guests to use.

What this looks like as a standing arrangement

Most of my STR owners are on a rolling annual: two formal cuts (late May and late August), one storm-response check in November after the first big blow of the season, and an on-call for anything unexpected. Recurring invoice, always the same schedule to the calendar, photo receipts on every job. Owner never has to remember it's happening. Cleaner has a hedge that never intrudes on turnover. Guests arrive to a front boundary that looks looked-after.
