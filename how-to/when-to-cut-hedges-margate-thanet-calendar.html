When to cut a Margate hedge, month by month.

The generic hedge-cutting calendar you find online was written for the Home Counties, not the Thanet coast. On CT9 the nesting-season law is the same, but the storm window, the salt-timing constraints, and the Easter Dreamland pre-season surge push the real calendar into a shape that's specific to this bit of coast. Here's the month-by-month I actually work to.

The two constraints that shape everything

Before the calendar, the two rules that override it:

  • 1 March to 31 August is the main bird-nesting window under section 1 of the Wildlife and Countryside Act 1981. Cutting a hedge with an active nest in it is an offence, up to £5,000 per nest. Blackbirds can still be sitting into October, so a September cut still starts with a visual check.
  • Coastal storm season runs September to February. Storm Ciarán put 85mph gusts across Kent on 1–2 November 2023. A hedge freshly reduced on the seaward face going into that pressure will regret it. Timing the heavier work so the plant has re-clothed before winter matters here in a way it doesn't inland.

The CT9 calendar

January

Winter maintenance window. Deciduous hedges (hornbeam, hawthorn, mixed native) can take a hard structural cut with the plant dormant. Avoid the seaward face on evergreen fronts, any salt-loaded January gale on a fresh cut face will scorch back regrowth in spring. Post-storm clearance if a November or December blow has broken standards or downed sections.

February

Same as January. Last month for hard structural work before nesting closes the window. If a hedge needs a proper renovation cut, mid-to-late February is your last realistic date.

March

Nesting window opens on the 1st. Formal maintenance cuts still permissible with a proper nest check first, but the whole tone shifts. This is also the start of the Easter Dreamland pre-season tidy for holiday-let owners, front boundaries wanting a light shape-up before guests start arriving. Light work only.

April

Easter Dreamland opens (typically early April). STR arrivals surge. A lot of pre-season tidy work happens now, light shaping, not reduction, always with a nest check. Last frost is usually mid-March on Thanet's H5 microclimate so new planting is possible from April onward.

May

Late May is the first big formal-cut window. By around the third week the earliest nests have fledged; a careful cut with a proper visual check on every metre of hedge is legitimate. This is the first of the two formal cuts for evergreen shaped hedges, griselinia, escallonia, yew, box, lonicera. Take the winter's growth off and set the line for summer.

June

Peak of the first formal-cut window. If May was rained out, June is when it happens. Nest checks still matter, some second broods are underway. By late June most formal hedges have been done.

July

Quiet month for planned cuts. Holiday season is at peak on the fleet, bookings are back-to-back, front boundaries are on show, but there's little you can do without disturbing guests or an active nest. Emergency work only (breakage, safety, gate access). New planting is a bad idea in a July heat run, Thanet's thin chalk topsoil dries out fast in the sunniest and driest corner of the UK (~1,577 sunshine hours a year).

August

Nesting window closes on the 31st. Late August is the ramp into the second big formal-cut window. If the plant is showing tidy growth and the visual nest check is clean, cuts from mid-August onward are usually fine.

September

Peak of the second formal cut. Full formal work, structural reductions on evergreen hedges, staged reduction year-two work, all the jobs that were held over from summer. This is my busiest month on the fleet. Still worth a nest check on the first few cuts of the month, blackbird stragglers can run into October.

October

Last of the second-cut window. Structural work on deciduous hedges begins as leaves start to drop. Storm season is now open, first Atlantic depressions arriving. Avoid heavy salt-face reductions on the seaward line from mid-October on.

November

Storm-response month. Storm Ciarán fell on 1–2 November 2023. This month often generates the year's emergency call-outs, downed sections, wind-broken standards, salt-scorched panels needing tidy. Planned deciduous work continues inland or in shelter.

December

Winter maintenance. Structural cuts on hornbeam, hawthorn, native mix. Post-storm clearance ongoing. Any planting on offer is bare-root, bare-root hedging is best planted November to March while dormant.

Salt-tolerant species timing quirks

Griselinia: two formal cuts, late May/early June and late August/early September, hold line well. Escallonia: same schedule but if you want the pink flowers, delay the first cut until immediately after they finish (usually mid-June). Elaeagnus: one late-summer cut usually enough; it grows slower than the others on the salt line. Sea buckthorn: prune only in winter, don't shape it during nesting, it's dioecious and you want the female berry-bearing plants to fruit.

The net curve, one paragraph

Late Feb: last of the winter structural work. March–April: STR pre-season tidy. Late May/June: first formal cut (evergreens). July: quiet, holiday peak. Late Aug/early Sept: second formal cut (the busiest window). October–February: storm response, deciduous work, planting. Nesting window shuts everything heavier than a light trim for six months. Storm window shuts everything on the seaward face for four. What's left is two tight windows, and if you miss them you wait.
