The August cut that is now against the law: what the 2024 hedgerow management rules changed.

Most garden hedges are completely outside the Management of Hedgerows (England) Regulations 2024. But if you manage an agricultural boundary in Thanet, the rules changed in May 2024 and it is worth checking where you stand. Here is what the regulations say, who they apply to, and what the changes mean in practice.

A common situation: a small arable landowner has cut a field boundary hedge every August for years with a tractor-mounted flail, then hears that new regulations are in force and August cutting is no longer allowed. The questions are whether the planned cut is now a problem, and whether getting a contractor to do it instead would change the position.

It is the same problem whether the landowner or a contractor does the cut: the rules also bar you from causing or permitting another person to cut. The Management of Hedgerows (England) Regulations 2024 came into force on 23 May 2024. They ban agricultural hedge cutting in the March-to-August window and require a 2-metre buffer strip alongside agricultural hedgerows. Here is what the rules say.

1. The qualifying test: is this an agricultural hedgerow?

The 2024 Regulations apply to hedgerows on agricultural land in England. Under GOV.UK guidance, that means a hedgerow growing on, or next to, land used for agriculture: horticulture, fruit and seed growing, dairy farming, and the breeding and keeping of livestock (including horses and ponies), along with grazing and meadow land, market gardens, nursery grounds and allotments. The rules also have a length test: the hedgerow must be more than 20 metres long (gaps of 20 metres or less count as part of it), or be shorter but meet another hedgerow at each end.

What is not covered by the cutting ban: a hedgerow within, or marking the boundary of, the curtilage of a dwelling house. If your hedge is between your garden and your neighbour's garden, the 2024 Regulations do not touch it. A hedge that is not on or next to agricultural land is also outside the rules.

In Thanet the places where the regulations do matter are broadly:

  • Field boundary hedges on arable land on the rural edge of Thanet
  • Boundaries of paddocks and grazing land
  • Market garden and smallholding boundaries
  • Any hedge where agricultural land sits on at least one side of the boundary

If you are uncertain whether your land qualifies, the Rural Payments Agency publishes GOV.UK guidance ("Hedgerow management rules: cutting and trimming") on which hedgerows are covered, and you can contact them directly.

2. The cutting ban: 1 March to 31 August

On qualifying agricultural land, the regulations prohibit cutting, trimming or flailing a hedgerow between 1 March and 31 August inclusive. This is the same calendar window as the accepted nesting-season guidance under the Wildlife and Countryside Act 1981 (more on the relationship between the two in step 5 below), but the 2024 Regulations operate independently and have a different mechanism. You must also not cause or permit another person to cut or trim.

The critical difference from the WCA 1981 approach is this: there is no nest-check exemption in the 2024 Regulations. Under the WCA 1981, a careful nest check is how you avoid committing an offence against nests in use. Under the 2024 Regulations, the ban on agricultural hedge cutting applies regardless of whether a nest check has been done and regardless of what it shows. A clean nest check does not authorise an August cut on agricultural land under these rules.

Exemptions do exist, but they are limited. You do not need to notify the RPA for most of them, but you must keep a record of the works and only cut the parts of the hedgerow that the exemption needs. The main ones are:

  1. Rights of way: a hedgerow overhanging a highway, road or footpath with a public or private right of way, where it obstructs or endangers users, blocks their view or blocks the light from a public lamp. A hedge that is simply inconvenient to cut in autumn is not an exemption.
  2. Dead, diseased, damaged or insecurely rooted hedgerow likely to fall onto a highway, road or footpath.
  3. Hedge-laying and coppicing between 1 March and 30 April, or trimming a newly laid hedgerow by hand within six months of laying.
  4. Serious plant, animal or human health risks, or a serious pest or weed infestation.
  5. Work by a statutory body acting under statutory powers, such as a utility company.
  6. Sowing oilseed rape or temporary grass in August: this needs notification to the RPA each year first, and you must still follow the Wildlife and Countryside Act 1981.

A routine August cut of a field boundary does not fall into any of those categories. The practical answer is to move the cut to September or later, once the ban has ended and the nesting window has reopened.

3. The buffer strip: 2 metres from the hedge centre

Alongside the cutting ban, the 2024 Regulations require landowners on qualifying agricultural land to establish and maintain a buffer strip of at least 2 metres, measured from the centre of the hedgerow, on any field side of the hedge.

Within that strip, three things are prohibited:

  1. Cultivation (ploughing, drilling, rotovating)
  2. Application of pesticides
  3. Application of fertilisers

You must also take all reasonable steps to establish and maintain a green cover within the buffer, whether naturally occurring or sown. The rules aim to protect hedgerows as habitat and wildlife corridors.

For a typical field hedge that is around a metre wide, the 2-metre measurement from the centre means the uncultivated strip extends roughly a metre and a half beyond the outermost branches. If the hedge is wider, the buffer extends correspondingly less. The key requirement is the measurement from the centre, not from the drip-line or the outermost branch.

The buffer strip rules were phased in: on land used for crop production they applied from the end of the first harvest after 1 July 2024, and on all other land they applied from 1 July 2024.

4. When the buffer strip does not apply

Some exemptions reduce the scope of the buffer strip requirement, though none of them affects the cutting ban. The two most relevant to Thanet landowners are:

Small fields (2 hectares or smaller). The buffer strip requirement does not apply where the field bounded by the hedge is 2 hectares or smaller in area. Many horse paddocks and smallholding enclosures in Thanet fall below this threshold. The cutting ban still applies to these hedgerows during the March-to-August window: the field size exemption removes the buffer strip obligation only.

Young hedgerows (less than five years old). Hedgerows established within the last five years are not covered by the buffer strip requirement. The regulations are targeting established field boundaries, not newly planted hedgerow schemes. Again, the cutting ban still applies from the moment the hedge counts as a hedgerow on agricultural land: new plantings should not be cut in the nesting-season window regardless.

5. Three legal frameworks, one hedgerow

The 2024 Regulations are the third layer of hedgerow law that can apply to agricultural boundaries in Thanet, and it is worth being clear about what each layer covers and what it does not:

Wildlife and Countryside Act 1981, section 1 covers active bird nests, regardless of whether the land is agricultural or domestic. It applies everywhere, at any time of year when nesting birds are present, and the March-to-August window is a practical guideline rather than a statutory date in the WCA itself. There is no calendar ban in the WCA itself: the offence is damaging or destroying a nest in use, so a careful nest check before cutting is how you avoid it. Blackbirds can still be active into October: the nesting-season picture for the Thanet coast is covered in detail at our nesting-season guide.

Hedgerows Regulations 1997 govern whether a qualifying hedgerow can be removed at all without giving 42 days' notice to Thanet District Council. The 1997 Regulations say nothing about cutting timing or buffer strips: they are solely about removal and whether the hedge meets the "important hedgerow" criteria. The tests and the TDC notice process are covered separately at our 1997 Regulations guide.

Management of Hedgerows (England) Regulations 2024 govern how agricultural hedgerows are managed: the cutting window and the buffer strip. They say nothing about domestic hedges or about removal. Enforcement sits with the Rural Payments Agency.

All three frameworks can apply to the same hedgerow at the same time. An agricultural boundary hedge in Thanet can simultaneously require a TDC notice before removal (1997 Regulations), be protected from nest disturbance during the breeding season (WCA 1981), and be subject to the August cut ban and the buffer strip requirement (2024 Regulations).

The practical message for anyone managing agricultural land in the CT7, CT8 or CT9 postcode areas: check all three frameworks before you touch an established field boundary hedge. The 2024 Regulations are the newest and the least well known. The RPA's GOV.UK guidance pages on cutting and trimming and on buffer strips are worth reading before the autumn cut season.
