The August cut that is now against the law: what the 2024 hedgerow management rules changed.

Most garden hedges are completely outside the Management of Hedgerows (England) Regulations 2024. But if you manage an agricultural boundary in Thanet, the rules changed in May 2024 and a significant number of landowners have not caught up yet. Here is what the regulations say, who they apply to, and what the changes mean in practice.

I got a call in early July from a customer near Westgate-on-Sea who runs a small arable holding on the chalk plateau above the coast road. He had been cutting the 300-metre field boundary hedge alongside his wheat ground himself every August for eight years: a two-day job with a tractor-mounted flail that he and a neighbour split between them. A farming contact had mentioned in May that new regulations were in force and August cutting was no longer legal. He rang to find out whether the cut he was planning was now a problem, and whether getting a contractor to do it instead would make any difference to his position.

It was a problem: the same problem whether he did it or I did it. The Management of Hedgerows (England) Regulations 2024 came into force on 23 May 2024. They ban agricultural hedge cutting in the March-to-August window and require a 2-metre buffer strip alongside agricultural hedgerows. Here is what those five rules actually say.

1. The qualifying test: is this an agricultural hedgerow?

The 2024 Regulations apply to hedgerows on agricultural land in England. Under these rules, agricultural land means arable land, permanent pasture, orchards and market gardens. Equine land (horse paddocks and grazing) also qualifies in most cases, as it constitutes land managed for agricultural purposes.

What is not covered: hedgerows growing entirely within the curtilage of a private dwelling where the land on both sides is domestic garden. If your hedge is between your garden and your neighbour's garden, the 2024 Regulations do not touch it. The same is true for hedges alongside car parks, roads, commercial yards and other non-agricultural settings.

In Thanet the places where the regulations do matter are broadly:

  • Field boundary hedges on arable land, particularly on the chalk plateau around Westgate-on-Sea, Birchington and the Garlinge flatlands
  • Boundaries between paddocks and grazing land on the rural fringe between Margate and Minster
  • Market garden and smallholding boundaries in the CT7/CT8 area
  • Any hedge where agricultural land sits on at least one side of the boundary

If you are uncertain whether your land qualifies, the Rural Payments Agency and the Farming Advice Service both publish guidance on which land categories are covered. Thanet DC's agricultural land classification maps are also publicly available and can confirm whether a given parcel is registered as agricultural grade.

2. The cutting ban: 1 March to 31 August

On qualifying agricultural land, the regulations prohibit cutting, trimming or flailing a hedgerow between 1 March and 31 August inclusive. This is the same calendar window as the Wildlife and Countryside Act 1981 nesting season (more on the relationship between the two in step 5 below), but the 2024 Regulations operate independently and have a different mechanism.

The critical difference from the WCA 1981 approach is this: there is no nest-check exemption in the 2024 Regulations. Under the WCA 1981, a competent visual nest check can clear a hedge for cutting inside the nesting window, provided no active nest is found. Under the 2024 Regulations, the ban on agricultural hedge cutting applies regardless of whether a nest check has been done and regardless of what it shows. A clean nest check does not authorise an August cut on agricultural land under these rules.

Exceptions do exist, but they are narrow:

  1. Urgent safety works: cutting to prevent immediate danger to people or property. A hedge that has blown across a public right of way or is blocking sight lines at a dangerous junction can be dealt with urgently. A hedge that is simply inconvenient to cut in autumn is not an exception.
  2. Statutory obligations: works carried out to comply with a court order or a requirement under another piece of legislation.

The Westgate customer's August cut did not fall into either category. He moved his cut to late September, once the legal window had reopened, and did the job in the same two-day way he always had.

3. The buffer strip: 2 metres from the hedge centre

Alongside the cutting ban, the 2024 Regulations require landowners on qualifying agricultural land to establish and maintain a buffer strip of at least 2 metres, measured from the centre of the hedgerow, on any field side of the hedge.

Within that strip, three things are prohibited:

  1. Cultivation (ploughing, drilling, rotovating)
  2. Application of pesticides
  3. Application of fertilisers

A green cover, whether naturally occurring or sown as a grass or wildflower mix, must be established and maintained within the buffer. The intention is to protect the hedgerow's root system from compaction and chemical run-off, and to allow hedgerow ground flora to establish alongside the base.

For a typical field hedge that is around a metre wide, the 2-metre measurement from the centre means the uncultivated strip extends roughly half a metre to a metre into the field beyond the outermost branches. If the hedge is wider, the buffer extends correspondingly less. The key requirement is the measurement from the centre, not from the drip-line or the outermost branch.

There was a transitional provision for fields that were already in crop production when the regulations came into force on 23 May 2024: if a field had no buffer strip and was growing a crop on 1 July 2024, the buffer strip requirement did not apply until that crop had been harvested. From the 2025 growing season onwards, the full requirement applies.

4. When the buffer strip does not apply

Two exemptions reduce the scope of the buffer strip requirement, though neither affects the cutting ban:

Small fields (2 hectares or smaller). The buffer strip requirement does not apply where the field bounded by the hedge is 2 hectares or smaller in area. Many horse paddocks and smallholding enclosures in Thanet fall below this threshold. The cutting ban still applies to these hedgerows during the March-to-August window: the field size exemption removes the buffer strip obligation only.

Young hedgerows (less than five years old). Hedgerows established within the last five years are not covered by the buffer strip requirement. The regulations are targeting established field boundaries, not newly planted hedgerow schemes. Again, the cutting ban still applies from the moment the hedge counts as a hedgerow on agricultural land: new plantings should not be cut in the nesting-season window regardless.

5. Three legal frameworks, one hedgerow

The 2024 Regulations are the third layer of hedgerow law that can apply to agricultural boundaries in Thanet, and it is worth being clear about what each layer covers and what it does not:

Wildlife and Countryside Act 1981, section 1 covers active bird nests, regardless of whether the land is agricultural or domestic. It applies everywhere, at any time of year when nesting birds are present, and the March-to-August window is a practical guideline rather than a statutory date in the WCA itself. A nest-check exemption exists under WCA 1981, allowing cutting inside the window if a competent visual check shows no active nests. Blackbirds can still be active into October: the nesting-season picture for the Thanet coast is covered in detail at our nesting-season guide.

Hedgerows Regulations 1997 govern whether a qualifying hedgerow can be removed at all without giving 42 days' notice to Thanet District Council. The 1997 Regulations say nothing about cutting timing or buffer strips: they are solely about removal and whether the hedge meets the "important hedgerow" criteria that can stop removal for five years. The tests and the TDC notice process are covered separately at our 1997 Regulations guide.

Management of Hedgerows (England) Regulations 2024 govern how agricultural hedgerows are managed: the cutting window and the buffer strip. They say nothing about domestic hedges or about removal. Enforcement sits with the Rural Payments Agency rather than with TDC or the police.

All three frameworks can apply to the same hedgerow at the same time. An agricultural boundary hedge in Thanet can simultaneously require a TDC notice before removal (1997 Regulations), be protected from nest disturbance during the breeding season (WCA 1981), and be subject to the August cut ban and the buffer strip requirement (2024 Regulations).

The practical message for anyone managing agricultural land in the CT7, CT8 or CT9 postcode areas: check all three frameworks before you touch an established field boundary hedge. The 2024 Regulations are the newest and the least well known. The RPA published a short explanatory video when the rules came into force, titled Management of Hedgerows Regulations (2024), with RPA field officer Peter walking through who the regulations cover and how the buffer strip works: it is available on the RPA's YouTube channel and is worth ten minutes of anyone's time before the autumn cut season.
