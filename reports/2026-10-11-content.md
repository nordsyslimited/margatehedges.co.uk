## Topic-gap check

Reviewed all 61 existing how-to articles. Cornus sanguinea (common dogwood) was not covered by any existing slug. The site already covers many native hedging species (hawthorn, blackthorn, hazel, field maple, bare-root native planting) but not dogwood specifically. Dogwood is distinct in three ways that justify its own article: it is one of the few native shrubs that demands an annual coppice cut for the principal visual value (winter red stems), it is specifically well-suited to CT9 chalk soils, and the cut timing sits across the late-February/early-March nesting-season boundary in a way that needs careful handling.
