Services · Tree work

Tree services in Margate — five standard operations, done properly.

The mature trees behind your hedge — the sycamores, ashes, holm oaks and Corsican pines that fill Thanet back gardens and holiday-let plots — need different work at different points in their life. I do the five operations that come up on almost every Margate garden: pruning, crown thinning, crown raising, crown reduction and deadwooding. Ash-dieback assessed as part of every quote on any ash. TPO and Thanet DC conservation-area applications handled where the tree is protected.

Pruning

Selective removal of dead, diseased or structurally unsound parts — branches, buds, roots. The general tree work every mature Thanet garden tree needs on a cycle. Formative pruning on young trees; maintenance pruning on established ones. £180–600 typical.

Pruning detail & pricing

Crown thinning

Selective removal of live branches to reduce canopy density. On the Thanet coast this is the honest first answer for anything that catches an easterly gale — wind passes through instead of loading the tree as a sail. £250–800 typical.

How thinning works

Crown raising

Also called crown elevation. Removing lower branches to give pedestrian, vehicle or building clearance, or to open a sightline. Common on Cliftonville front-garden limes and Westbrook driveway sycamores. £220–600 typical.

When to raise

Crown reduction

Reducing the overall height or spread by cutting branches back to lateral limbs of a sensible size. Not topping — that's the wrong approach and I don't do it. On a holiday-let plot this is the sensible pre-season call before a mature tree grows into the property. £350–1,200 typical.

Reduction vs topping

Deadwooding

Removal of dead, dying or diseased branches from the canopy. Purely for safety and tree health — nothing structural changes. On the salt-loaded windward side of a coastal tree, dead limbs accumulate faster than inland; annual deadwooding is often the right cycle. £180–550 typical.

Before we cut

TPO and conservation-area checks — included.

Central Margate and most of Cliftonville sit inside one of Thanet District Council's conservation areas — Margate (1978, extended 1994), Margate Seafront (1997), Northdown (1985), plus the Cliftonville cluster of Dalby Square, Clifton Place & Grotto Gardens, Ethelbert Road & Athelstan Road, Edgar Road & Sweyn Road, Norfolk/Warwick/Surrey Road and Northdown Road. Any tree over 75mm in diameter at 1.5m inside a CA needs six weeks' section-211 notice to Thanet DC before work. A tree with a Tree Preservation Order needs a full application with 8 weeks' notice. I check both, file the paperwork, and quote knowing which window we're working in — no last-minute stop-work order on the day. Fine for cutting a CA tree without notice runs up to £20,000 per tree.

On coastal-facing plots the salt-load matters as much as the CA rules: the windward third of any Thanet tree ages faster than the leeward two-thirds. That shows up as dieback in the crown, brittle wood on the seaward limbs, and heavier deadwood yield year on year. I plan the cut window around it — late summer for beech and hornbeam where they've been used inland-style, dormant-season for the salt-battered sycamores and limes.
