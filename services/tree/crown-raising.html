Tree services · Crown raising

Raising the crown : clearance without wrecking the outline.

Crown raising (also called crown elevation) removes the lower branches of a tree to give clearance underneath. Pedestrians can walk under it, holiday-let guests can wheel a suitcase up the drive, delivery vans can reach the door, the render on the terrace next door stops taking scrapes, or the sightline to the sea opens back up. Done right, the tree above still looks like a tree.

When raising is the right call

  • A mature front-garden lime or sycamore on a Northdown or Cliftonville terrace whose lower branches are dragging on the pavement and forcing walkers into the road
  • Vehicle-height clearance over a Westbrook or Garlinge driveway, the standard target is 2.5m for a car, 4.5m for a delivery van, 5.2m for a road
  • Holiday-let driveways where a suitcase, buggy or wheelchair needs level clearance to the door, a common brief on the Airbnb-heavy Cliftonville streets
  • Sightlines at a junction or at the top of a drive where a tree has grown into the line of view
  • A tree leaning on the render of a house or holiday-let annex, lower limbs off, then re-check whether a reduction is also needed higher up

How high to raise

A rule of thumb: after raising, the live crown should still be at least two-thirds of the total tree height. Take too much off the bottom and the tree looks bare-legged; it's also mechanically weaker in a wind because the wind load on remaining branches has increased. On the Thanet coast that last point matters more than inland, an over-raised tree is a lever arm for the easterly gale to work on. If the maths says the tree needs to lose more than a third of its live crown for the clearance you want, a raise is the wrong tool, you probably need a reduction, a fell, or to change the target height.

Species notes

  • Lime, sycamore, London plane, the classic raise candidates. All three respond well and grow into a good shape above.
  • Holm oak, raises cleanly and the evergreen canopy above still reads as full. Good candidate on inter-war Thanet front gardens.
  • Ash, assess for dieback first. If the crown is compromised, a raise removes the healthiest limbs and leaves the risky ones up top; wrong tool.
  • Cedar, monkey puzzle, ornamental conifers, a raise fundamentally changes their form. Owners often regret it. I'll flag it before we cut.
  • Fruit trees (apple, pear), often raise-and-thin together for orchard picking access on the Garlinge and Northdown back-garden plots that still have working fruit trees.

Pricing

Single-tree crown raise in Margate typically runs £220 – £600. Roadside or over-driveway work sometimes needs a small MEWP (cherry-picker) hire for one day and coning of the road, which adds cost; I call this out on the quote rather than absorb it into a vague day rate.

Before we cut

Same as all tree work, Thanet DC conservation-area and TPO check first. If the raise takes the crown lower than it was measured at when the TPO was made, that changes the paperwork window. I do that check as part of the quote.
