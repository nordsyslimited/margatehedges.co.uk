Service · Hedge cutting

Routine cuts done properly, on every species that copes with Thanet salt.

The bread-and-butter job: twice-yearly cuts on hedges already at a good height. Done right, this is what keeps a Cliftonville or Old Town front boundary looking like it belongs to the house.

What's included

  • Top, sides and front face cut to your chosen height and width
  • All arisings cleared and disposed of properly, Manston Road HWRC (CT9 4LX) or Richborough (CT13 9NW) under my EA upper-tier waste-carrier registration (£154/3yr)
  • Brush-down of the path, pavement or driveway underneath, important on tight Cliftonville terrace fronts
  • Six-week section-211 notice filed on your behalf if you're in a Margate-area CA and the hedge has any stems over 75mm at chest height
  • Fixed price quoted in advance, no day-rate creep

Pricing

Most routine cuts in Margate fall in the £120 – £400 range depending on hedge length, height, and how much access I need. Cliftonville terrace boundaries sit at the lower end (short but fiddly on-street parking); Westbrook and Garlinge boundary hedges at the upper. Fixed price after photos and postcode, no surprises on the day, no charge if the quote isn't accepted.

Timing by species : Thanet-coast net curve

The best window depends on what you're growing and where it sits relative to the salt line. My default cadence:

  • Formal front boundaries (Griselinia, Escallonia, Elaeagnus, privet, Leylandii, hornbeam), two cuts a year: late May / early June (post-nest-check) and late August / early September
  • Pre-holiday-let tidy for STR owners, rolling Jun–Aug turnover trims fit either side of the main cuts
  • Yew, holly, single late-August cut; a hard clip once a year, resentful of more
  • Cherry laurel, late summer; bruises easily, so secateurs and loppers on visible faces where time allows
  • Native mixed (hawthorn, blackthorn, hazel), winter only (September to February) to comply with nesting law
  • Storm-damage clearance, Sept–Feb baseline; Thanet caught Storm Ciarán 2023 badly, so this is a genuine seasonal beat

What I don't do on cutting jobs

If a hedge needs more than a routine cut, overgrown, gappy, leaning, salt-burned across the top, that's a reduction job, not a cut. I'll tell you that at quote stage rather than do a botched cut and pretend it solved the problem.
